# A place in one another's lives

Character and story direction for Café Hygge, 7 September 2026.

**Status: largely accepted by the owner as the working creative direction.**

ambition below when planning new work. Detailed biographies, dialogue, scene

Acceptance does not mean every recommendation is immutable or already shipped.

## 1. The story we are telling

Lunafreya comes to a city where nobody knows her and makes a place where people
can feel at home. Gradually, she discovers that she is allowed to feel at home
there too. The café begins as something she must make work. It becomes somewhere
she can occasionally sit down.

The story should let the player answer three questions over many reading
sessions: **What brought her here? Who are these people when they leave the
café? What have they begun to mean to one another?** Those answers accumulate
through conversations, habits, practical projects and objects with histories.

The neighborhood has no central emergency. People can disagree, feel lonely,
miss somebody, make an awkward joke or change their minds. Warmth comes from
how they treat one another through those moments. Most visits remain ordinary.
A tender scene should be surrounded by cups, weather, books and someone trying
to retrieve a pencil from under the cat.

Three strands develop together:

- **Lunafreya's own life:** her past, preferences, boundaries, family and possible
  romance; the apartment lets us see how a conversation stays with her.
- **The regulars' lives:** projects and relationships that matter to them,
  including matters Lunafreya does not direct or resolve.
- **The place they share:** furniture, books, music, food and the changing view
  become occasions for recognition. Their traces persist in both rooms.

The first long arc can reach a satisfying resting point: Lunafreya invites
someone upstairs, chooses what to call her café, and joins a small gathering as
one of its guests. Life continues afterward. Neither a romance nor a large,
fully furnished café is necessary for that emotional arrival.

## 2. Lunafreya: a definite past, room for the player within it

### Recommended backstory

Place Lunafreya around thirty. For several years she worked in a bookshop with
a small café and an events room in another city. She knows coffee, stock,
closing routines and how to welcome somebody who has arrived alone. This gives
her visible competence a believable origin.

She became the person who covered a shift, arranged a reading, remembered the
milk order and stayed to clear the cups. There were friends and good evenings.
Nobody had to be cruel for the life she had built to become too small for her.
She was good at making room for other people's plans and had stopped making
many of her own.

The turning point was an offer to coordinate events across several branches.
Everyone assumed she would be delighted. She realized that she did not want
the next several years to be a larger version of the same life. She initially
accepted, then withdrew before starting. That awkward act of changing her mind
was her first deliberate step toward the café.

Opening somewhere of her own was an old, half-private idea. She spent months
saving, learning the practicalities and looking at premises. The affordable
listing with an apartment above brought her to this city. On her viewing visit
she sat by the water with a takeaway coffee and found herself imagining an
ordinary Tuesday there. She knew nobody locally; the physical move and opening
still happened within the few exhausting days already established in the game.

Her savings bought a modest beginning. There is no concealed inheritance,
financial rescue or incoming debt plot. Family and old friends still exist.
In particular, her sister Calandra loves her but initially mistook the move for
Lunafreya wanting less of their shared life.

The deeper truth arrives gradually: she wanted a welcoming place, and she
wanted permission to want something. Both existing first-conversation answers
are true ways of approaching that history. The player decides what she admits
first, how she understands it now and what kind of life she makes next.

Give her ordinary pleasures and edges as well as that uncertainty. With Calandra
she argues cheerfully with crossword clues and imitates the old shop's closing
announcement. She remembers a customer's name more easily than the name of
the street she lives on. She can become briefly impatient with a stubborn
drawer, initiate an outing, dislike a suggestion and make someone laugh. The
player should get to enjoy her company as well as understand her decision.

### What the player authors

Keep concrete historical facts consistent. Do not ask the player to select a
bereavement, former career or abandoned relationship from a menu halfway through
the story. Let choices establish taste, privacy, emphasis and future decisions.

For example, she can tell Kasper that changing her mind felt mostly frightening
or mostly relieving. A later reference remembers that answer. She can tell
Gerda the story today or say she would rather talk about the blanket. Deferring
leaves another natural opening later; it does not cancel the relationship.

The existing `luna-beginning-*` and `luna-cafe-*` choices retain their exact
meaning. They influence the first callback, never become personality classes,
and never prohibit a later wish for both books and neighbors.

### How the player learns her history

| Opening in ordinary life | What she can reveal | What follows in the present |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holger asks how she learned to run a café | Bookshop work, event evenings, liking the people who stayed afterward | The bookshelf has personal meaning beyond storage |
| Nico notices a moving box still labeled with the old city | The move took months to decide and days to carry out | At home she unpacks an ordinary mug she kept avoiding |
| Birgit asks about a familiar closing habit | She often covered everybody else's last task | She admits what she actually likes to eat, rather than choosing for guests |
| Kasper talks about revising a decision | She accepted the promotion and then withdrew | A remembered answer colors a later scene about changing the café's plans |

These are recommended first tellings, not eight compulsory keys. Essential
history needs alternate openings with an established friend, so someone who
does not pursue Nora, buy an oven or install a piano can still understand
Lunafreya. A home object can support a recollection, but patrons remain the main
way she talks about her past. Track what she has disclosed, and to whom: one
person must not know a private conversation merely because the player saw it.

## 3. The ensemble

### Names and the city they belong to

The city takes inspiration from Copenhagen and Christianshavn, including their
mixture of people and backgrounds. Danish names belong alongside international
and unusual literary names. The owner's reference list is inspiration, not a
required selection or a constraint on the cast's genders or origins.

| Earlier name | Working name | Role and naming intention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Søren | Tomas | The builder; a grounded, understated name alongside his practical voice |
| Poul | Marcel | The painter across the lake; distinct from Nora and easy to recognize in conversation |
| Mikkel | Nico | The delivery person; short, informal and comfortable in a recurring greeting |
| Signe | Saira | The musician; from the owner's list, with an identity that extends beyond music |
| Rikke | Elody | The gardener; drawn from Elody du Bois in the reference list, using a given name in ordinary café dialogue |
| Malthe | Ezra | The young adult student and possible helper; from the owner's list |
| Freya | Antonia | The evening reader; from the owner's list and clearly distinct from Lunafreya |
| Ida, the sister | Calandra | From the owner's list; an unusual, expansive name that sits naturally beside Lunafreya |
| Asta, the librarian | Ida | Retains a name the owner likes in another suitable part of the community |

Holger, Gerda, Nora, Kasper and Birgit retain their names. Calandra and Lunafreya
can have an ordinary family history with parents who liked elaborate names;
their names need not imply a fantasy setting or an elaborate naming mystery.

When writing the fuller histories, include both longtime residents with varied
family backgrounds and people who arrived more recently. Give that mixture
ordinary presence through family relationships, languages people use with one
another, books, visits and particular memories. A name alone establishes no
nationality, religion, accent or immigration history. Those details should be
authored deliberately as part of a whole person, and each person can also be
funny, established, contented and simply at home here. Lunafreya is not the only
person who has moved, and moving need not mean the same thing to everyone.

The rest of this proposal uses the revised names. The existing evening reader
is still displayed as **Freya** in the shipped game; **Antonia** is the planned
display-name change for that same person. Preserve her `freya` save identity,
habits and history when that change is implemented. This is parallel to keeping
Nora's legacy internal ID while using her proper displayed name.

New regulars need distinct silhouettes, small habits and preferred orders with
simple-menu fallbacks. Their preferred equipment is never the condition for

### Holger — someone to leave a book with

His thirty years on the Kattegat remain the center of his working history.
Retirement took away the effortless company of people who happened to be in
the same room. His arc is learning to arrange company rather than hoping it
will occur. Do not add a dead spouse or a disaster at sea.

The first continuation recalls Lunafreya's actual opening answer. He offers
books once there is a shelf, or introduces the idea while talking about the
neighbors. The box contains an adventure novel, a battered cookbook and a
gardening book that clearly did not belong to a ship's technical library.
He knows which crew member recommended each one.

Later, he admits that an old shipmate lives close enough to visit and that
neither has suggested it. Lunafreya can offer the café as a meeting place or
encourage a simpler walk. He makes the invitation himself. A later scene lets
her join them for a cup instead of serving throughout.

His lasting changes are a personal bookplate, an occasional shared table and
references to meetings that also happen elsewhere. His fond dispute with Gerda
about where to sit predates Lunafreya. He and Marcel recognize the same harbor
from different working lives.

Voice sample: “There are six books. Seven if you count the one holding the box shut.”

### Gerda — asking what someone wants

Keep the shipped cat scarf intact. The next knitting project is for Lunafreya:
a lap blanket, with a remembered choice of **reeds or little stars**, and later
a choice of where to use it. Gerda asks casually, works over several café days,
and eventually presents exactly what was chosen.

For her deeper history, this proposal deliberately recommends that Erik was
her late husband. He was sociable, incapable of folding a fitted sheet and
quite convinced he knew better than she did about planting beans. His absence
has a place in her life; she also has current friends, bad television she
enjoys, projects and things to look forward to.

One evening she admits that she still plans some projects around what he would
have liked. This leads to making something in a color *she* loves. Lunafreya
does not make her move on. They can simply agree that a familiar pattern and a
new one can both belong in the same room.

Her small flaw is deciding what others need too quickly. When Lunafreya names
a preference, Gerda follows it without wounded feelings. She also asks Elody
for a cutting for her current balcony, linking the garden she once kept with
somewhere she lives now. Ida supplies cheerfully disreputable novels; Gerda
refuses to pretend they are for somebody else.

Voice sample: “The cat has excellent taste. Unfortunately, it is mostly in wool.”

### Nora — including the person who made the room

Preserve both shipped paintings and their existing locations and save IDs.
New writing can explain her earlier grand rooms as commissions: spaces whose
owners often asked her to remove the evidence that anybody used them. She
still accepts some commissions and enjoys parts of that work. The café gives
her a different subject, with people who can answer back.

Her arc begins with noticing a paint stain or the changed window light. She
asks what Lunafreya would want a painting to remember: the room before opening,
or a particular ordinary moment with someone in it. Later Nora discovers she
has repeatedly painted Lunafreya only as a pair of hands behind the counter.
She asks permission to make a different picture.

This becomes a conversation about being useful and being visible. Lunafreya
can choose a public café picture, a private apartment study or decline being
portrayed while keeping their friendship. Nora learns to ask instead of
assuming close attention is always welcome.

Marcel and Nora trade practical observations about pigments, drying and light.
Neither is the superior kind of painter. Kasper frustrates her by asking for
the title of an unfinished picture; she does the same to his book later.

Possible romance: fascination becomes mutual curiosity, then ordinary ease.
The meaningful turn is Nora putting the sketchbook away to spend time with
Lunafreya without making anything of it.

Voice sample: “Could you leave that cup? No, not forever. I know you need cups.”

### Kasper — letting something be read

Keep chapter seven, the laptop and the cautious pleasure of three good lines.
Recommend that he previously published a short, funny story that people liked.
His current novel has become burdened with proving that this was not a fluke.
There is no cruel reviewer or secretly stolen manuscript to defeat.

First he permits a conversation about writing without showing any. Then he
asks whether Lunafreya likes endings that arrive quietly or leave a door open.
Much later, he offers a paragraph shaped by that answer. She is a trusted first
reader, never responsible for solving his plot or choosing the correct career.

He and Ida disagree amiably about whether a book should explain itself. Birgit
reads the page and asks a concrete question his literary friends missed. A
small café reading, if chosen, has four listeners and a joke he gets wrong the
first time. He can finish a modest piece while the novel remains alive.

Gift: a hand-bound little story with a personal dedication. Its ending can
reflect the remembered choice. He asks before using a recognizable detail of
Lunafreya's life in public work.

Possible romance: they learn to say things before composing the perfect version.
It must continue after the confession into an ordinary life with someone whose
laptop is sometimes closed.

Voice sample: “I fixed the first sentence. It has caused problems for the second.”

### Antonia — someone with whom nothing needs explaining

Keep her evening visits, familiar book and comfort by the fire. Recommend that
she drives a city bus. She knows the street from early shifts and late runs;
the café is one of the few stops at which she can stay as long as she likes.
Her contentment is real. It does not conceal a mandatory tragedy.

Her scenes concern rereading, favorite stretches of road and the pleasure of
being unremarkable somewhere. She and Lunafreya can exchange a book without
requiring a report afterward. Eventually she offers to show her a little place
by the water; the invitation waits for a chosen outing, presented as a brief
authored scene rather than a new explorable district.

Nico knows the same streets through doorbells and stairs. Their competing
descriptions of one address become a running joke. Ezra shows Antonia a bus
map; she points out that his beautiful new route goes through the lake.

Gift: a postcard of an ordinary local stop with a short note on the back.
Her deeper contribution is letting Lunafreya be quiet without treating it as
a request to be cheered up.

### Nico — invited to stay

The same Nico handles the bookshelf and later deliveries. He knows where a
door sticks, folds packing blankets carefully and remembers the cat. Furniture
has proper transport and work reservations; a piano uses appropriate additional
help rather than making him carry it alone.

At first he drinks standing up, coat on. He is used to people being pleased
that something arrived, then forgetting who brought it. He likes his job and
has friends elsewhere, but has fallen into treating every visit as a stop.
The meaningful recurring gesture is a second cup for which he has time.

After the first introduction he can come off duty. Never require repeated
purchases to unlock his story. He and Tomas already work together sometimes;
he knows Birgit's early-morning routine and trades route observations with Antonia.
Privately he takes photographs of small doorways and shop windows, mostly on
his phone, with no ambition to launch another artistic career.

Gift: a printed photograph of the café on one of its early delivery mornings,
with a later matching view. Its importance comes from someone noticing that
she was building a life while she thought she was only assembling furniture.

Possible romance: practical familiarity turns into a deliberately chosen visit.
The first date begins with him arriving without a parcel, checking whether she
has time and staying through a silence he usually fills with a joke.

Voice sample: “Delivery for Lunafreya. The cat has already signed for the wrapping.”

### Tomas — work that belongs to somebody

Give the existing window worker this recurring identity without replaying
completed repairs. He remains the familiar contact for later carpentry and
building jobs, with specialists when a job needs one. Marcel is the established
painting specialist in that small network.

Tomas notices hinges, uncomfortable handles and what happens to furniture after
people have lived with it. He is proud of useful work and quietly irritated by
catalogue photographs that put tables where nobody can walk around them.
He is also a very enthusiastic, not especially successful bread maker.

His personal project is a small cupboard for his adult daughter's new flat.
He has been overengineering it because he wants the first thing he makes for
her own home to last. She likes the simple version. He decides to believe her.
That story gives Lunafreya another angle on making a place perfect before
letting anybody enjoy it.

He begins visiting without a toolbox. Birgit gives advice about his bread only
after he asks. Nico teases him about packing more spirit levels than sandwiches.
Gift: a small wooden coat hook or frame made from an agreed offcut, offered for
the apartment. Friendship does not provide free major upgrades or discounts.

Voice sample: “It's level. The wall has a different opinion.”

### Saira — a tune that can have company

Saira accompanies a community choir and teaches piano. She enjoys both; this
is not a story about an artist who must be rescued from teaching. She is good
at following other people's timing and rather less practiced at making space
for something of her own.

Introduce her as a guest before there is a piano. She taps a rhythm lightly,
then stops because someone nearby is reading. A conversation establishes that
she likes music which leaves room for the room. She can mention a used piano
and its practical needs without turning its purchase into a friendship test.

Once an instrument is installed and prepared, her first invited scene is a
few exploratory notes. Lunafreya can prefer something bright or something
gentle; the answer affects an eventual short composition, not the global volume.
Saira develops it during ordinary visits. The personal first sharing waits.

Later she asks Lunafreya to join her for a few notes, compatible with the
protagonist's existing private piano habit. Listening is an equally complete
answer. A small music evening brings in Kasper, Nora and some quiet listeners;
it does not become a concert business or applause minigame.

Without a café piano, the friendship and personal history continue through
conversation and a handwritten score. The physical playing scenes wait for a
usable instrument. All music respects existing mute and volume choices.

Gift: the framed score and, after the attended sharing, the option for its
quiet motif to join ordinary piano repertoire. Possible romance centers on
shared attention, playful rehearsal and two people learning each other's pace.

Voice sample: “The cat has chosen a key. Several, actually.”

### Marcel — the person in the view

Make Marcel the existing house painter across the lake. He paints buildings for
a living. His first café visit puts a familiar faraway figure close enough to
have a name. He has watched her changing windows as she watched his wall.

His first line must acknowledge the actual facade state: still working, nearly
finished, or completed long before this introduction. Preserve the original
`street-house` progress and its attended finishing beat. If it is already
complete, the introduction refers to that past job; it never restarts it.

The facade was one commission, not his only purpose. Later he is painting
somewhere else and returns for coffee, or handles the café's chosen wall finish.
He can show two small samples and remember her preference if she later books
the work. She remains free to change the actual finish in the planner.

His emotional thread is watching familiar places change and discovering that
being recognizable does not require remaining identical. An old shop sign he
painted has been replaced. He misses it, yet likes the new occupants. Lunafreya
can talk with him about why she chose this city and what she would keep if the
café grows.

Gift: a small painted view of the café from his side of the water. He is friendly
with Holger, exchanges technique with Nora and remembers Gerda's former garden.
The painter in the distant scene and the visiting person share one presence;
he must never visibly work across the lake while sitting inside.

Voice sample: “I thought your window was getting larger. Turns out you were taking the boards off.”

### Birgit — having breakfast in somebody else's place

Birgit runs a nearby bakery. She likes her work, has opinions about buns and
occasionally tells a story with the punchline much too early. Her first visit
is an ordinary breakfast after her morning work. The café can be a place where
she is served, without becoming a rival business plot.

She and Lunafreya compare closing habits, then tastes. A remembered citrus or
spice preference leads to a recipe card and a tasting. An oven later allows
the recipe to become part of actual preparation and visible food stock. If no
oven is bought, the shared tasting and friendship still happen.

Her developing wish is to try one new recipe without replacing the familiar
favorite everybody orders. Both can coexist. Her connection with Tomas is
comic, practical and friendly; she reads Kasper's story without performing the
role of a literary critic.

Gift: the handwritten recipe, with real notes and a correction in the margin.
No critical ingredient, food supply or profitable menu is locked behind praise
for her baking. Recipe preference changes flavor and appearance, not an optimal
earnings multiplier.

### Elody — something that can be shared

Elody tends an allotment and has an unreasonably detailed memory for local
weather. She brings a small cutting once they know each other. She asks where
it might fit rather than announcing that the café needs more greenery.

Her arc concerns deciding how much of an abundant plot she actually wants to
manage. She chooses to share part of it with another gardener because she
wants company, not because she has failed. Gerda offers a remembered planting
tip; Ezra becomes interested in something that will not follow his diagrams.

The plant can go to the café or apartment. A later terrace makes another scene
possible, but her story remains available indoors. Ordinary watering and
healthy growth continue autonomously; no neglected-plant sadness.

Gift: the cutting in a hand-labeled pot. Later, a small group of plants in the
room quietly records exchanges among several people. Elody is allowed scenes
that are simply funny conversations about slugs.

### Ida — a shelf people are allowed to use

Ida works at the library, likes a well-labeled shelf and reads some extremely
melodramatic novels. She arrives out of interest in the café's books, but can
also arrive with her own book before the shelf exists.

She offers to help organize the exchange. Lunafreya can prefer loose browsing
or little handwritten recommendations. Ida follows that choice. Her personal
arc is noticing that improving a shared space sometimes means resisting the
urge to manage it. Gerda removes one unnecessarily stern instruction from a
draft sign; Ida laughs and writes a friendlier one.

She introduces people through books without announcing their private histories.
Kasper can choose to let her put his little story on the shelf. A quiet shared
reading afternoon allows everyone to read a different book; nobody gives a report.

Gift: a handmade stamp or set of bookplates reflecting the chosen café name
once one exists, with an unnamed version available beforehand. She does not
control access to ordinary books or require returns to be tracked.

### Ezra, Calandra and the cat

**Ezra** is a young adult student, around twenty-two, with a notebook full of
imaginary transit maps and plans for an adult life he thinks ought to be more
definite by now. Antonia punctures his beautiful impossible routes kindly; Elody
lets him help with something real and small. His friendship begins well before
any possible job. If the shared-work milestone eventually ships, he is the
recommended first helper candidate, with his own studies and off-duty habits.
Choosing not to hire him leaves a complete recurring friendship. Employment,
housing and romance are separate decisions; he is not a proposed romance route
in this first ensemble.

**Calandra** is Lunafreya's sister, living in the old city. A letter or PC message
can wait quietly among evening activities. She remembers versions of Lunafreya
the patrons have never met: funny, occasionally impatient, capable of wanting
something inconvenient. An invited visit makes the old and new lives meet.
She need not confess to being a bad sister. They can admit they misunderstood
each other and eat the absurd amount of food she brought. Her visit does not
require expensive furnishings or a partner.

**The cat** remains one animal across both rooms, with no human dialogue. It
joins the stories by choosing Nico's wrapping, sitting on Kasper's warm
laptop, wearing Gerda's existing scarf and interrupting a careful musical pause.
It is also present in private apartment moments without being used to explain
every emotion. Any future naming choice should be its own small, optional beat.

## 4. Stories that belong to more than one person

The cast needs relationships that are visible when Lunafreya is not talking.
Use occasional shared seating, a nod, an exchanged book or a small caption.
Important changes still wait for an attended invitation. Do not turn the room
into simultaneous dialogue windows.

| Shared arc | Sequence across visits | What remains afterward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The first borrowed shelf** | Holger's offer → Nico delivers an empty shelf → Holger presents a small collection → Lunafreya shelves it in spare moments → Ida helps shape the exchange → Antonia or Kasper finds something to read | Working browsing, Holger's bookplate, a remembered organizing preference, people recommending to one another |
| **Your side of the water** | Tomas repairs the window → Lunafreya notices Marcel → they meet → Nora and Marcel compare the same light → Lunafreya chooses a detail to preserve in a later picture | A pair of views, a personal connection to the exterior, comments appropriate to actual room changes |
| **A blanket for this room** | Gerda asks about a pattern → knitting grows → a conversation about Erik if welcomed → attended gift → Lunafreya places it → Calandra later recognizes that her sister's home has acquired other people's kindness | Chosen blanket, small home routine, Gerda receiving care as well as giving it |
| **Something for four listeners** | Saira develops a motif → Kasper offers a short passage → Nora makes a small program if she is known → Lunafreya chooses music, words or both → a few invited guests gather | New ordinary music/reading habits and a keepsake, without making the café louder or busier by default |
| **A recipe with two handwritings** | Birgit's tasting → Lunafreya's preference → a recipe card → optional oven and autonomous baking → Tomas's bread story supplies a running joke | A real recipe variant and evidence of their friendship; no oven required for the personal arc |
| **Come upstairs** | Lunafreya chooses to invite Calandra, a friend or a partner → objects already placed influence what they notice → she lets a guest make the tea → they settle somewhere modest | The apartment feels shared with her life downstairs; visits remain possible without cohabitation |
| **A name people already use** | Several conversations collect what the place means → Lunafreya chooses an authored café name or keeps the current one → a small sign scene → neighbors begin using it | Sign and appropriate later references; Fleur de Lune remains a candidate, never an imposed name |

Not every listed character is required for a shared arc. Write a small core
scene and optional contributions. Ida's involvement should enrich the shelf
without making her a prerequisite to Holger's gift. Nora's program must not
block Saira's music. Separate invitations and ordinary visits avoid contrived
requirements for six people to be present at once.

Let connections also produce mild, repairable friction. Ida's labels can feel
too formal to Gerda. Saira's rehearsal can overlap with Antonia wanting quiet;
they agree on a softer passage or another time. Nora may ask a personal question
too directly and accept a boundary. Characters can disagree without an approval
meter, an argument minigame or an apology quest for the player.

## 5. How the mechanics carry the story

### Practical progress and personal moments

Keep this distinction explicit: **approved practical work can finish while the
player reads; a personal revelation, gift handover or first sharing waits.**

For the shelf, for example:

1. Holger makes an optional offer. It is remembered and never expires.
2. The bookshelf can be purchased through ordinary planning regardless of the
   first dialogue answer. Nico delivers and installs it autonomously.
3. The empty furniture exists, but browsing remains unavailable.
4. Holger's invitation returns on a normal visit. Accepting his small collection
   is an attended gift moment, with a brief reference to Lunafreya's bookshop past.
5. After acceptance, shelving is ordinary interruptible work. Browsing starts
   when usable books are actually shelved; the box need not be completely empty.
6. Purchased book boxes use the same stocking work. They remain available if
   the player never takes Holger's invitation, and can fill the larger collection.

If the shelf is already stocked, Holger contributes a few distinguished titles
and a bookplate without exceeding capacity or overwriting purchases. If books
are accepted without available display space, the collection is safely kept
until there is room. An established full café should receive an appropriate
continuation, never a replay of being empty.

Delivery and repair jobs must finish and release their work areas even when
their worker has something personal to say. A pending conversation follows the
person into an off-duty visit. Do not make a worker stand at the door forever
or require another paid booking to speak to him.

### Choices that have a future

The accepted direction is recorded in [the narrative contract](../narrative.md#6-conversations-and-the-shape-of-branching).
Choices are never
graded and never punish absence; they may meaningfully select what happens,
what is made and which relationship develops. A single save need not contain
every mutually exclusive variant. Core friendships and ordinary equipment
remain available without finding a correct answer.

| Kind of choice | Example | Later evidence | Can it be revisited? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Preference | Reeds or stars on Gerda's blanket | Actual motif, gift dialogue and a later recognition | The finished gift retains its history; new commissions can differ |
| Self-description | The move felt more frightening or relieving | A friend remembers the precise emphasis | Later dialogue can acknowledge that feelings changed |
| Privacy | Tell Nora why she left, or keep the conversation on painting | Nora respects that boundary and does not repeat undisclosed facts | Another suitable opening remains available |
| Shared activity | Silent reading afternoon or a small reading aloud | Different attended gathering and ongoing callbacks | Another type of gathering can be chosen later |
| Presentation | Put a portrait at home, display it, or keep it stored | Actual location and who has seen it | Freely move or store it later |
| Relationship | Name an outing as a date or as friendship | Distinct scenes and relationship expectations | Changing direction is an explicit later conversation |
| Household/work | Invite somebody to stay or offer Ezra a job | Additional autonomous routines | Separate, clearly explained later decision; never bundled with a gift |

No arbitrary hidden affection thresholds. Familiarity comes from actual shared
moments and time, with readiness visible only as a quiet invitation. Preferences
do not accumulate into a secret personality score. A single flirtatious answer
cannot silently commit Lunafreya to a relationship.

### Gifts that become part of the rooms

Use a small set of authored locations: picture hooks, a shelf space, a windowsill,
the reading chair, bedside area and desk. Each keepsake supports the locations
that make physical sense; a blanket cannot be hung in a picture slot. The
evening planner can offer a quiet **keepsakes** view for placing or storing them.
Do not require a freeform decorating system or a collectibles checklist.

The character presents the gift only in an attended scene. The player can
choose a supported café/home location or keep it safely for later. Once that
choice is made, Lunafreya carries and places it through an ordinary saved task.
Store who gave it, its chosen variant and a short memory. Objects are never
silently discarded because the room is full, and nobody becomes upset if a
gift is put away. Display choice is not an affection test.

These gifts do different work: Holger's books enable an ordinary activity;
Saira's music joins a repertoire; Gerda's blanket changes a resting place;
Nora's picture changes what a wall remembers. Avoid issuing one decorative
trophy at the end of every friendship. Some payoffs are simply a new shared
habit, an invitation or a person arriving without their work equipment.

### Pacing a large cast in a small café

Keep existing admission and seat limits. More people in the writing catalogue
means variety across days, not a larger crowd every afternoon. Introduce the
five existing regulars properly before bringing everyone new through the door.
Nico and Tomas can enter naturally through the next practical milestones.

Use lightweight rotation for later arrivals, preserving early familiar faces.
People have broad morning, afternoon or evening habits, not appointment timers
the player must watch. A ready story can modestly favor its owner's next
eligible visit, with fairness so other people still come. No one must repeatedly
buy something, change their real reading hours or catch a random overlap.

A character offers one conversation at a time. Several ready beats stay behind
that invitation, ordered by continuity. After a major scene, allow a later visit
before another substantial reveal; returning after a long absence should not
produce a marathon of confessions. Ordinary conversations and silent visits
remain between major beats. No counters show a growing backlog.

## 6. Romance and a life after the confession

Long-term candidates: **Nico, Nora, Kasper and Saira**. Their different rhythms
offer practical companionship, close observation, tentative verbal intimacy
and playful shared attention. Each needs a complete friendship regardless of
romance. Develop one full route first rather than four incomplete promises;
Nico is the recommended first prototype because he connects naturally to
deliveries and already needs a separate off-duty life.

A route grows through several ordinary shared scenes before a clear invitation
to a date. The player can choose friendship, say not yet or accept. Friendly
choices stay specific and warm. The other person has tastes, plans and boundaries
of their own; romance is not a reward for agreeing with everything they say.

The important scenes come after acknowledging attraction: a first deliberately
unproductive evening, a small misunderstanding about privacy or time together,
a conversation that resolves it, and an ordinary morning in which the intimacy
is visible without a declaration. Examples include Nico sitting with his
coat off, Nora leaving an unfinished sketch untouched, Kasper making tea before
opening the laptop, and Saira enjoying a silence she need not accompany.

Recommend one mutually agreed committed partner at a time. Casual early interest
does not lock other routes. Commitment is explicit and describes its effect;
it does not turn other candidates into jealous rivals. A later change of heart
gets an honest scene and a respectful friendship where appropriate, with no
revenge, financial loss or vanished keepsakes. Deferred invitations stop
resurfacing as romantic hints after an explicit friendship boundary, unless
the player later chooses to reopen that subject.

Moving in is a separate, much later mutual choice. The partner brings belongings,
uses the home, leaves for their own life and has routines that are not all about
Lunafreya. Dating does not automatically provide café labor. Hiring does not
grant access to romance, and a temporary guest is not required to repay housing
with affection. A contented single life and deep platonic household possibilities
deserve authored endings of equal warmth.

## 7. Dialogue, scenes and emotional texture

Use ordinary exchanges of roughly 6–12 short bubbles, deeper conversations of
12–20, and rare set pieces of about 2–4 attended minutes. These are writing
targets to test, not forced timers. Most scenes need zero or one consequential
choice. Give Lunafreya authored lines, humor and questions of her own.

Keep the established camera, speaker-attached canvas bubbles and manual
continuation. A chosen reply is actually spoken. Pausing, Settings, hiding the
page or reloading must preserve both the conversation and its obligations.
Later scenes are optional; the first Holger/home tutorials remain the only
existing mandatory exceptions.

Suggested set pieces reuse the café, apartment and visible waterfront:

| Scene | Small physical action | Emotional purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holger's box | Open a box, read an inscription, leave one book on a table | The first improvement carries somebody's history |
| The old mug | Lunafreya unpacks it after a relevant conversation; the cat settles on discarded paper | Her old and new lives can inhabit the same room |
| Two sides of the water | Marcel sits at the window while Nora shows a study of his view | Scenery becomes a relationship |
| Gerda's own color | She holds up something she made for herself | Care can include the person who usually gives it |
| Four listeners | A few chairs turn; Saira plays or Kasper reads; someone shifts to make room for Lunafreya | She participates in the place she made |
| Calandra upstairs | Calandra puts food down and notices one genuinely placed keepsake | Her sister sees the life she could not explain before |
| A first date | A visitor arrives without their usual reason for visiting | Spending time together becomes a named choice |
| A place at the table | Lunafreya sits; someone else pours; the cat takes the inconvenient remaining space | The first long arc rests in belonging |

Special gatherings are booked as **ready when you want to begin**. They do not
occur at an unattended clock deadline. Start from a safe service/home transition
and arrange only a few participants along real routes. Once the scene begins,
normal obligations wait under the attended-moment rules. A set piece needs
saved semantic checkpoints for setup, conversation, choice, result and return.
If postponed, everyone can resume ordinary life without losing the invitation.

A little sample of the shelf conversation's intended voice:

> **Holger:** I nearly kept the cookbook. There was something in it I meant to try.
>
> **Lunafreya:** What was it?
>
> **Holger:** I haven't decided. I've only had it twenty years.
>
> **Lunafreya:** We had one like that in the shop where I worked.
>
> **Holger:** You worked with books?
>
> **Lunafreya:** Books, coffee, readings. Mostly finding enough chairs.
>
> **Holger:** Did you enjoy it?
>
> **Lunafreya:** A lot of it. I think that's why leaving took so long.

The scene does not have to resolve that admission immediately. Holger can ask
which shelf the adventure book belongs on. A later conversation can return to
what she said. Let some emotionally important lines remain plain.

## 8. A feasible sequence of releases

These are working content releases, not in-game levels. The next engineering
milestone remains the bookshelf. [First books, first connection](first-books.md)
splits the first release into three reviewable passes: Nico's empty-shelf
delivery, usable purchased books, then the attended character conversations.

| Release | Complete player-facing result | Preparation at that boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. The shelf has people attached** | Nico delivers an empty shelf; purchased books stock it separately; Holger's next optional scene offers a small collection and reveals Lunafreya's former work | Delivery reservations, separate furniture/contents, stable IDs for the new conversation, resumable gift and stocking effects |
| **2. Familiar faces become people** | Proper introductions for Gerda, Nora, Kasper and Antonia; Tomas acquires a name and an off-duty visit; first backstory callbacks | Extract general conversation execution before multiplying bespoke scenes; migrate positional history deliberately |
| **3. Things worth taking upstairs** | One selectable Gerda blanket, supported placement in either room, one apartment recollection and Calandra's first message | Saved gift variants, placement anchors, recipient knowledge, resumable home moments |
| **4. The neighborhood meets itself** | Marcel's visit, Ida and Elody introductions, book exchange and one shared reading gathering | Identity across worker/patron/exterior roles, fair visit rotation and small group scene staging |
| **5. A tune for the room** | Saira before and after an optional piano; delivery/setup, motif preference and an attended first sharing | Instrument capabilities, safe preparation work, bounded music and pause/reload checkpoints |
| **6. A life she can describe** | Birgit's arc alongside the first proven recipe; promotion revelation, Calandra's visit, optional naming and the first long arc's quiet gathering | Essential-history fallback scenes, continuity review across purchase orders and known cast |
| **7. Choosing someone** | One complete romance including ordinary life after the first date, then additional routes in small releases | Explicit relationship choices and complete friendship alternatives |
| **Later: sharing the work or home** | Ezra as a real possible colleague; separately, a partner/guest with their own home routine | Claimable work, station ownership and two-person home behavior, as required by the scalability audit |

Verification automation and save export/import still belong before substantial
public play, as the [scalability audit](../scalability-audit.md) recommends. Do
not defer those protections until players have months of authored relationships.

For a first substantial story collection, budget approximately **40–55 attended
scenes** across the first five releases, with most attention on the established
cast and a few complete new introductions. Later expansion toward this full
ensemble could reach roughly **90–120 scenes**, including cross-character
moments and romance variants. These are editorial scope estimates, not a
promise of playtime or a reason to write every route before testing one.

Each character packet should include a handful of ordinary reusable exchanges,
4–6 substantial scenes for a core arc, 1–2 connections with other characters
and behavior after the apparent ending. A character who has received a payoff
must not revert forever to the same line from their introduction. Write and
play one complete packet before mass-producing dialogue.

## 9. The small technical contracts this ambition needs

Follow the audit's guidance to make a few boundaries explicit. Keep the
dependency-free runtime, existing simulation and shared rendering path.

- **Identity:** one durable character identity can appear as a worker, patron,
  distant figure or home guest, with one active physical presence. New identity
  data does not rename Nora's legacy `lunafreya` ID or existing arc flags.
- **Conversation:** use stable scene/node/choice IDs; explicit prerequisites,
  speaker knowledge and continuation; keep text separate from the executor.
  An example such as `holger-books / old-shop / answer` survives inserting a
  new earlier sentence. Historical positional flags need an explicit mapping.
- **Consequences:** commit selected replies before acknowledging them and apply
  scene results once. Receiving a gift, adding books, changing a relationship
  and queuing installation must survive a reload between those steps without
  duplication or loss.
- **Progression:** share existing improvement definitions and book capacity
  rules. Check actual installed capability, not whether the player has heard a
  particular suggestion. Personal objects without free anchors stay stored.
- **Story readiness:** prerequisites refer to actual shared moments and facts,
  plus patient running-café time where useful. Pending invitations never expire;
  alternate scenes prevent an optional upgrade or unused romance from holding
  the protagonist's essential history hostage.
- **Scheduling:** one fair, bounded arrival path; work reservations released
  independently of invitations; safe entry/exit and explicit ownership of
  instrument, seat and work area. No duplicate patron/worker versions of Nico.
- **Memory:** distinguish a world fact, Lunafreya's chosen description and what
  a particular person has been told. Save stable outcomes rather than a complete
  transcript. Extend the versioned schema with migrations as fields are added.
- **Authoring:** ordinary script-loaded data files can be split by character or
  story collection. A small validation tool should catch missing node targets,
  unreachable endings, undefined gifts, impossible prerequisites and effects
  with no safe repeat/reload behavior. No universal visual story editor needed.

Test the observable combinations that matter: both existing opening answers;
books bought before/after Holger's offer; no shelf and an already full shelf;
unmet or absent secondary characters; no piano; a worker leaving with a pending
invitation; gift acceptance and placement interrupted by reload; privacy and
romance deferrals; each motif variant; established saves with every existing
arc complete; a long unattended run with all new payoffs waiting. Add one real
day/home/next-morning path for each new shared routine. Keep browser sessions
owned, disposable and cleaned up under the repository's normal workflow.

## 10. Editorial decisions and success criteria

The owner largely accepted this plan and its revised naming direction. Its
working assumptions include Lunafreya's bookshop/café past and withdrawn
promotion, Calandra as her sister, Erik as Gerda's late husband, the expanded
cast and Nico/Nora/Kasper/Saira as possible adult romance routes. These remain
specific, editable writing decisions rather than a requirement to implement
every biography or romance exactly as first drafted.

The story bible records concise planned direction alongside shipped facts;
this document keeps the longer arcs and cross-character reasoning. Before a
release, write only its needed scenes and settle their exact facts, choices
and consequences in the implementation brief. When it ships, update the bible
and roadmap with what actually landed. Keep unshared personal history out of
ambient lines and other characters' knowledge until the relevant scene permits it.

Judge the result by a few observable questions:

- Can the player explain why Lunafreya moved without having dated somebody or
  bought every improvement?
- Does each recurring person want something outside Lunafreya's story, know
  somebody besides her and remain recognizable in an ordinary silent visit?
- Can a remembered choice be seen in a later line, object, activity or explicit
  relationship, without becoming a test with a best answer?
- Does a glance at a bookplate, blanket, picture or empty extra cup recall a
  moment the player chose to share?
- Does the small café remain warm for somebody who postpones every new scene?
- After a character's first arc resolves, does their ordinary life continue?

The emotional destination is modest and concrete: one evening Lunafreya looks
around the apartment and recognizes objects she did not bring when she moved.
She remembers who gave them to her. Downstairs, there are people who recognize
her footsteps. Tomorrow need not contain an event to feel worth coming back to.
