# Fleur de Lune — story and lore inspiration

This is a creative reference for the `game` branch: a collection of echoes,
questions and possible moments to return to while discovering the game. It is
not a fixed plot, a roster of promised characters or an implementation checklist.
[overview.md](overview.md) sets the direction; [game.md](game.md) records what
exists. Unless identified as part of the opening, scenes below are possibilities.
The retained companion café's arcs are not automatically Lunafreya's history.

At the center is an ordinary woman making an ordinary place matter. Lunafreya
owns Fleur de Lune; her blonde hair is gathered in a bun, and she has opinions,
appetites and a life beyond being useful. Nora belongs to the original companion
café and is not the player here.

## Core themes

**Care makes possibilities visible.** Cleaning the first window changes what
Lunafreya can see and what passersby can see of her. A room becomes somewhere
someone might enter. Restoration can accompany an emotional change without
every repaired object needing to stand for a wounded person.

**What does it cost to build something worth having?** Money matters, but so do
an afternoon, a difficult request, patience with someone else's method, or the
pleasure of doing everything oneself. Investment can be joyful. Shared work
can give energy back; exhaustion need not prove devotion.

**Belonging grows through repetition.** Someone learns where the spoons are.
Someone notices that Lunafreya has not sat down. A familiar person orders
something different, and she asks whether they like it instead of assuming she
already knows them. Being seen includes being allowed to change.

**Growth changes the shape of home.** More space can welcome new lives and unsettle
old routines. A beloved café need not remain tiny, and expansion need not be a
fall from grace. The question is how its people make room for one another.

**Life includes unfinished things.** A friendship can become less close without
being a failure. A painting can remain unfinished because its maker found
something more interesting. Stagnation may end through curiosity, not crisis.
Joy, purpose, solitude and intimacy need not arrive in a prescribed order.

## Lunafreya: the moon and the lady

Her name offers a poetic reading: **Luna + Freyja, the Moon + the Lady**. Treat
this as an imaginative association, not a certified etymology of the combined
name or a settled explanation of why her parents chose it.

Luna suggests moonlight, night, reflection and cycles: the same room looking
different without becoming a different room. Lunafreya may be recognizable
through changing moods. She need not be equally sociable every day to remain
herself. Reflected light also suggests receiving: the warmth she offers can be
warmth she has received from others.

Freyja's associations with beauty, love, fertility, gold, magic, strength,
grief and desire offer a wider emotional palette than gentleness alone; her
name means “Lady.” These are mythic associations, not traits Lunafreya must
embody. [Background on Freyja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freyja).

In this human life, growth might mean learning a craft or making room for a
friend's ambition. Beauty can be something Lunafreya actively wants, even when
it serves no practical purpose. Desire includes romance, a lovely lamp, privacy,
or wanting the café to become bigger than others expect. Strength might appear
as a firm refusal. Gold might be warm light on her hair, a brass fitting brought
back to a shine, or something precious she chooses to keep for herself. The
magical association can live in how astonishing a changed room feels after
weeks of comprehensible work.

She is not secretly divine, chosen, supernatural or destined. A later creative
direction could revisit that boundary, but none of these echoes establishes it.
Her grief, if explored, does not need a mythic counterpart; her blonde hair is
not evidence of ancestry. Nor must she be serene because her name sounds poetic.
She can be stubborn about a paint color and forget where she put the brush.

Different people hear different names:

- A mythology enthusiast hears Freyja and asks which version of the story she
  likes. Lunafreya might know very little about it, or dislike their favorite.
- A child hears the moon and asks whether she is allowed to stay up all night.
  “Allowed, yes. Good at it, no.”
- A romantic hears an invitation to elaborate compliments. She might enjoy one
  and gently puncture the next: “You can just ask whether I'm free on Thursday.”
- A botanist thinks of a plant for the window; an artist notices how gold hair
  and cool glass divide the light in a portrait.
- A historian wonders how a name travels between languages. Lunafreya is more
  interested in why three different people spell hers differently on parcels.

Their readings reveal the speakers. None has final authority over who she is.
Leave her family history and her own relationship to her name open to discovery.

## Fleur de Lune: a place that opens

The café's name evokes a flower of the moon. It answers Lunafreya's name through
imagery rather than translating it: the woman and the place share light, but
the café will gather meanings she did not put there herself. Leave the precise
story of choosing the name open. She may eventually tell it differently to
someone she knows well than to someone asking across the counter.

Two botanical images offer distinct possibilities. Moonflower (*Ipomoea alba*)
opens toward evening; lunaria (*Lunaria annua*, honesty) has flowers followed by
round, silvery seed pods. Lunaria is not another name for that night-opening
vine. One suggests opening when others close; the other, beauty that changes
form after flowering. [Moonflower, Missouri Botanical Garden](https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/PlantFinder/PlantFinderDetails.aspx?cv=5&isprofile=0&lo=1&taxonid=279304);
[lunaria, Royal Horticultural Society](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/98034/lunaria-annua/details).

These might enter as dried stems, a botanical drawing, a gifted plant or an
ambitious future garden experiment. They need not make the café nocturnal.
Daylight, lunchtime chatter and a thoroughly unpoetic delivery belong here too.

The opening already supplies a vocabulary: remove rubble, sweep, repair a
window, restore light, place two chairs, serve Holger. Later scenes can return
to these actions with changed company. Once Lunafreya carried a chair in alone;
later someone holds the door while she complains about its weight. The echo is
stronger if nobody explains it.

## A builder's price

In the story told in *Gylfaginning*, the builder of the gods' fortification asks
for Freyja, the sun and the moon as payment. It is a useful, extravagant image
of a building price that reaches beyond money. [Hurstwic's account of the wall
of Asgard](https://www.hurstwic.org/history/articles/mythology/myths/text/wall_of_asgard.htm).

Let the game borrow the question, not the bargain's treatment of a person as
payment. A patron might object to that part of the tale before Lunafreya can.
Nobody's affection is owed in exchange for repairs, generosity or a room key.

Quiet versions of the builder's price could recur:

- A carpenter asks to be paid fairly, while a friend offers an afternoon freely.
  Both forms of contribution deserve respect; community does not mean free labor.
- Lunafreya accepts help repainting, then has to accept that another person's
  careful work does not look exactly like hers.
- A useful expansion would occupy the corner where she spends her own quiet
  mornings. She wants both things and has to think, not discover a correct answer.
- A regular admits that volunteering was easier than asking for company.
  They can still enjoy the work, and next time simply ask to stay for tea.
- Someone tells the myth during a repair. “The sun and the moon? Mine wanted
  the ladder back by Friday.” Later, the same joke might introduce an honest
  conversation about a promise that became too large.

Sacrifice should be chosen and proportionate, never the universal admission
price for love. Sometimes the wise investment is postponing a room, sharing
responsibility or keeping an evening free. These are story tensions, not absence
penalties, exhausting meters or demands that the player sacrifice real time.

## Old stories rhyming with present lives

Susan Beckmann's “Java to Geneva: The Making of a Pratt Poem” transcribes
working notes for E. J. Pratt's *The Truant*, published in 1942. On printed page
11, “Freya (Venus) blonde” appears near “Luna”; a subsequent draft also links
Freya with golden blond imagery. This is a documented juxtaposition in a
scholarly transcription, not evidence that Pratt coined Lunafreya's name or
connected these words into a single identity. [Beckmann's article, PDF](https://ojs.library.ubc.ca/index.php/canlit/article/download/194574/190406/230197).

The creative invitation is the pleasure of stumbling across something familiar
in someone else's unfinished thoughts. A poetry-loving patron could bring a
copy of the article, delighted by a coincidence Lunafreya finds mildly funny.
“You found someone misspelling me before I was born.” They might go on to talk
about drafts, abandoned ideas and why a crossed-out line can feel more intimate
than a finished poem.

Fictional traces can carry the same feeling: an old poetry book with a pressed
flower, a previous occupant's moon sketch beside a shopping list, several
attempts at a sign on the back of a receipt. These are invented café objects,
not Pratt artifacts. Their makers may remain unknown. A familiar motif need
not become a clue, a prophecy or a mystery with a final explanation.

## Recurring motifs to vary, not decode

| Motif | A possible presence | How its meaning might change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silver and gold | Cool window light beside a warm lamp; dried lunaria beside brass | At first, outside and inside; later, two people sharing the same table from different lives |
| Moon and reflection | A pale shape in wet glass, a cup or an artist's sketch | Being watched becomes seeing oneself differently; sometimes it is just a reflection |
| Flowers and growth | An opening bloom, a cutting, dried stems kept over winter | Anticipation becomes sharing, then keeping something beautiful in another form |
| Repairs and seams | A patched chair, mismatched hardware, an old paint edge | Evidence of scarcity becomes evidence of contribution without making new things less worthy |
| Two places to sit | The first chairs; a stool brought over; a table moved apart | Welcome becomes intimacy, disagreement, privacy or room for someone new |
| Thresholds and keys | A repaired door, a hesitant arrival, a key entrusted to a helper | Entry becomes participation, then shared responsibility with agreed boundaries |
| Hands at rest | Sanding, pouring, exchanging a cup, finally putting down a cloth | Competence becomes trust that the room can hold itself for a moment |

Use a few at a time. Weather need not agree with emotion: excellent news can
arrive in heavy rain; a difficult goodbye can happen in lovely sunshine.

## Relationships: people with lives of their own

Familiarity can begin with an order and grow into mutual recognition. It also
needs room for surprises. A quiet regular might be wonderfully loud with an
old friend. Someone content with their life can bring curiosity, affection or a
very serious opinion about biscuits without concealing a wound.

Lunafreya participates rather than dispensing solutions. A person can want a
listener, practical help, a distraction, privacy or disagreement taken seriously.
She may ask which. Others can notice her frustration and help her; she may have
to apologize when her wish to be useful becomes interference.

Let friendships, chosen community, possible romance and creative partnerships
have their own value. Intimacy can mean sitting without performing cheerfulness,
being teased accurately, or being trusted with a first attempt. It does not
always require confession, romance or a declaration of permanent closeness.

People can want incompatible things: a quiet reading corner and rehearsals,
professional ambition and a familiar routine, a closer friendship and more
distance. A compromise may leave disappointment. Some disagreements persist
alongside affection; some people leave. Departures and returns belong to authored
life changes, never punishment for the player's absence.

Keep the humor specific. A confident amateur baker can produce an excellent
cake and an absurdly bad label. A serious reader can maintain a petty feud with
a chair. Lunafreya can laugh too hard at something that would not sound funny
retold. Not every exchange ends in wisdom, and not everyone speaks in metaphors.

## Focused conversations: permission to be present

The closer camera is a change in attention. Meaningful conversation suspends
demanding play: orders, work and urgent timers wait. The café continues through
quiet ambient life, and lingering cannot cost progress. Avoid making tenderness
compete with an interface asking the player to get back to work.

Give this space to a first honest request, a shared success, an apology with
something at stake, a boundary, an unexpected laugh, or comfortable silence that
would once have been awkward. Save room for joy; closeness is not an alarm that
someone is about to reveal suffering.

The place can help a conversation become memorable:

- **At the repaired window:** a friend asks whether Lunafreya actually wants
  their help. Rain taps behind them; her hand stops polishing the same spot.
- **At the first table:** two people taste something successful. Cup sounds
  and a badly suppressed grin carry the pause before either claims credit.
- **On an unfinished room's threshold:** a helper asks for a say in the space
  they will work in. The occupied café is audible behind them; neither has
  stepped fully into the new room yet.
- **Beside a future garden window at dusk:** companions wait for a flower.
  One talks about moving away, then asks an ordinary question about watering.
  The plant need not bloom on cue to validate the conversation.

Revisit locations. A window once associated with asking for help might later
frame an easy disagreement. Keep pixel gestures, breathing, light and quiet
sound legible; avoid swelling every important moment into a revelation.

## The café as a record of a life

Restoration uncovers earlier lives, and use leaves new traces. The room should
gradually contain both. A repaired table can retain its old cup ring and acquire
a drawing made here yesterday. A new, unscarred chair can become meaningful
because someone chose it carefully.

Possible traces include a photograph taken before the room was ready, a regular's
gift displayed in an unexpected place, a handwritten recipe with two different
hands correcting it, a book returned with a train ticket as a bookmark, or a
plant cutting that later appears in several pots. A lost glove may simply be
reclaimed. Leaving something behind does not make every patron a mystery.

Objects can complicate affection. Lunafreya dislikes a gifted vase but likes its
giver. A drawing portrays her as more confident than she feels. A visitor asks
to remove an old photograph after their life changes. Care sometimes means
moving, returning or letting go of an object rather than preserving it forever.

Seasonal arrangements can reveal who was involved: a practical winter curtain,
a spectacularly uneven paper garland, dried seed pods replacing fresh flowers.
Spaces acquire names through use, perhaps “the troublesome table” long after
its wobble is fixed. Environmental details should reward noticing without
requiring a collectible hunt or a speech explaining each one.

## Story seeds and recurring dialogue

These are starting situations, not ordered quests or predetermined endings.
Any new named character can emerge later from the game's Danish naming tradition.

**The chair nobody agrees about.** A repair-minded patron offers to remove an
ugly patch; its previous owner prefers it visible. Lunafreya discovers that she
likes the patch but dislikes being told that keeping it proves good character.
A later conversation can begin, “Does it still squeak?” rather than reopening
the whole argument. Restoration raises taste as well as memory.

**The portrait with the wrong expression.** An artist paints Lunafreya looking
tranquil. She asks whether she really looks that way while calculating supplies.
The artist admits they painted how the café makes them feel. She may sit again,
keep the imperfect portrait, or ask for something less flattering and more hers.
Being seen is different from being made into someone's reassuring image.

**A perfectly good Tuesday.** Two regulars invent increasingly impractical names
for a perfectly ordinary bun. Lunafreya joins in. Weeks later a scribbled name
still makes them laugh. The event needs no hidden pain or reward beyond their
new shared joke.

**The borrowed afternoon.** Lunafreya asks someone to watch the café while she
does something for herself. On returning, she finds the cups arranged differently
and everything otherwise fine. The consequential question is whether she can
leave them there. “Did you need me?” can slowly become “How was your afternoon?”

**A place for the noise.** A future music evening delights some patrons and drives
a reader home early. Both have a reasonable claim to belonging. Another room
might offer possibilities, but listening and changing arrangements matter too.
Nobody needs to be converted into liking the same things.

**The moonflower that takes its time.** A botanist and an impatient enthusiast
bring sketches, compare observations and get distracted talking. The hoped-for
bloom becomes an occasion for companionship, not a timed event the player can
miss. Later, dried lunaria on the same sill may provoke an affectionate argument
about which kind of beauty requires less fuss.

**The person who is doing well.** A patron gets an opportunity elsewhere and is
excited to leave. Lunafreya is pleased and will miss them; neither feeling cancels
the other. A postcard can eventually share an ordinary success, without promising
a return or turning the empty seat into a permanent shrine.

**Holger at the old table.** If their relationship develops beyond the implemented
first cup, Holger might return to that table after an expansion. Lunafreya asks
whether he preferred the café small. “I liked it then. I like this chair now.”
He may have a more complicated answer another day. The first visitor need not
become the guardian of an unchanging café.

Useful recurring questions can enter through practical situations: What are we
keeping? Did you want advice? Who chose this? When does a place feel like yours?
What can you give gladly? Does being needed feel the same as being welcome?
Let answers change, let someone decline the question, and let many conversations
remain about lunch.

## As the café expands

These are possible changes of emphasis, not a mandatory progression ladder.

**One restored room** makes self-trust tangible: can Lunafreya turn intention into
a place where someone can sit? Light crosses a newly cleaned surface. Accepting
the first guest makes private effort visible to another person.

**A working neighborhood café** brings recognition and competing preferences.
Recipes, repeat orders, useful equipment and objects chosen by others let
management carry relationship stories. Lunafreya can enjoy earning and upgrading
without every purchase becoming a test of virtue.

**Adjoining rooms** ask whose purposes fit inside her original idea. A reading
room, workshop or music space can give someone else creative ownership. New
thresholds give old motifs new uses; a shared key means more than a larger map.

**Helpers and a broader menu** make trust practical. Someone might want training,
fair pay or freedom to attempt a specialty. Their ambition may eventually take
them elsewhere. Lunafreya can discover which parts of the work she loves and
which she is relieved to share.

**A courtyard or garden** offers another pace of growth and another kind of
gathering: cuttings exchanged, plans revised, someone content to watch. Seasons
can change the scene without decay or missed-care punishment.

**A beloved gathering place** raises questions of continuity. Newcomers should
not need years of shared memories to belong. The café can keep evidence of its
first life while allowing new traditions, larger projects and Lunafreya's own
changing desires. Being its owner need not become her entire identity.

## Open space

Leave unanswered why Lunafreya acquired this particular building, who occupied
it before, whether she wants romance, which friendships endure, and how large
Fleur de Lune becomes. Do not imply a lost inheritance, a devastating past or a
single destined partner merely to fill the gaps.

Return to the room, the people and what they are doing together. A useful new
story may begin with a grand old myth, but find its life in someone holding the
other end of a shelf, asking for the window seat, or staying because the company
is good.
